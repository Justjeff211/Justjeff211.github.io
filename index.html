About

I don't just study security.
I build it.

My path into cybersecurity didn't start in a classroom. It started with a need to understand how systems actually break. Reading about attacks wasn't enough, so I set out to stand up the infrastructure, generate the traffic, trigger the alert and walk the investigation myself.

I'd rather spend a weekend building a hybrid Azure lab and chasing down a detection gap than memorise a slide deck about one. Every project here exists because I built it, broke it, monitored it and wrote down what happened. That is the same loop I want to run inside a SOC.

Critical Half day

Conti Ransomware Investigation

A full SOC investigation into a Conti ransomware compromise of a Microsoft Exchange server.

28,145 events analysed in Splunk 8.2.2 across Windows Security, Sysmon and IIS logs to reconstruct the complete attack chain from raw log evidence.

The trail ran from initial exploitation of three CVEs through to a trojanised cmd.exe planted for persistence, which is how the attacker kept access after the vulnerabilities were patched. Written up as a 24-page technical report with 24 annotated screenshots.

Tier 1 Simulation 8 days

Threat Detection & Log Analysis

A Tier 1 SOC investigation simulation built on Apache access logs in Splunk.

112 Apache access logs ingested into Splunk and analysed with 9 custom SPL queries to identify suspicious HTTP behaviour and botnet-style traffic patterns.

The work is documented as a formal incident report: what was searched, what the results meant, and which patterns separated ordinary traffic from automated abuse. Eight days logged, the largest single time investment of any project here.

Identity Attack Half day

Legacy Account Recovery, ATO Case Study

A SOC case study on identity-based attacks, inspired by the 2013 iCloud breach.

The study maps a full account-takeover attack chain to MITRE ATT&CK and NIST, phase by phase, then writes a SOC detection rule for each phase rather than stopping at the description of the attack.

The point was to practise the translation step: taking a documented historical breach and turning it into something a SOC could actually alert on today.

Case Study

When an alert becomes an investigation

ScenarioConti Ransomware, Exchange Compromise
SeverityCritical
EnvironmentMicrosoft Exchange Server
Detection SourceSplunk 8.2.2, 28,145 events
01 · Initial Access
Exchange server exploited

Three CVEs identified as the entry point: CVE-2020-0796 (SMBGhost), CVE-2018-13374 and CVE-2018-13379.

02 · Persistence
Trojanised cmd.exe

A modified cmd.exe was planted to maintain access even after the exploited vulnerabilities were patched.

03 · Correlation
28,145 events reviewed

Windows Security, Sysmon and IIS logs correlated in Splunk to reconstruct the full attack chain.

04 · Mapping
Methodology documented

How the attacker maintained access after patching, mapped step by step from the log evidence.

05 · Reporting
24-page technical report

Findings written up with 24 annotated screenshots supporting every step of the investigation.

The investigation: a full SOC investigation into a Conti ransomware compromise of a Microsoft Exchange server, using Splunk 8.2.2 to analyse 28,145 events across Windows Security, Sysmon and IIS log sources. The trail ran from initial exploitation of three CVEs through to a trojanised cmd.exe used for persistence, reconstructing the complete attack chain from raw log evidence.

The outcome: a 24-page technical report with 24 annotated screenshots documenting the exploited vulnerabilities, the persistence mechanism, and how the attacker maintained access even after the vulnerabilities were patched.
